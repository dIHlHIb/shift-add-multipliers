## Checks that could have failed

The small residue oracle computes all signed representative additions from scratch. It covers every raw residue pair, including 0, repeated targets and targets congruent to the input, for periods 3, 5 and 7 and budgets 0 through 5. The fixed larger two-helper controls have both positive and negative cases. The mutation experiment deletes repeated parents, removes subtraction, or forbids output-to-output use in the tail. Each mutation is tested against real positive circuits. A one-bit shift error is also injected into an integer witness and a Lean example. [Small oracle](src/small_oracle.py) · [Cut controls](src/cut_controls.py) · [Mutation tests](src/adversarial.py)

The two-source-product hardware measurement tests all 65,536 signed input words against ordinary integer multiplication, at every output bit. Both Hcub and six-gate candidates use the same arithmetic implementation and mapping scripts. The Hcub graphs preserve their parents while their gate representations are normalized by the same generator. Critical-path and area minima can come from different designs. [Selection and mapping](src/panel.py) · [Bit-exact checker](src/check_products.py)

The experimental side enumerates up to 500 six-gate label sequences per pair before choosing circuits to map. The ceiling matches the number of distinct Hcub candidates sent to ABC. Preselection search effort and solver run time differ. The delta measures circuit quality under this design-selection procedure.

Tracing both output cones backwards removes one dead addition in 76 of the 27,232 FFT trials. The corrected best-of-32 baseline misses by one addition on 59 sets (172 positions). The sole affected graph in the hardware panel gives identical measured area and delay after remapping both flows. A separate search allowed every acyclic integer realization using at most four helpers from one Hcub trial, then from the union of all 32 trials, including labels at dead gates. Rewiring one seed closes four of the 59 gaps. Pooling the 32 label sets closes 15 in total and leaves 44 gaps (132 positions). Every new positive graph is replayed in exact integers. The 29-block hardware comparison drops unused gates before mapping. The next section describes how both sides choose their parent nodes. [Rewiring search](src/rewire.py) · [Per-set results](results/hcub-rewiring.json)

## Matched hardware comparison

In the first comparison, only the six-gate search could reassign parent nodes. The matched comparison applies the same parent-selection and ranking rules to its candidates and to Hcub’s. The primary control also matches the number of different intermediate-label sets and the number of mapped designs per side, per instance. Two further comparisons use all available candidates and then let Hcub choose from its original and rewired designs. The candidate-generation algorithms and their computation times still differ. With equal candidate counts and parent-selection rules, the 29 FFT blocks save 4.38% in total area under the standard flow and 4.29% under resyn2. The two FIR examples use Hcub’s original parent choices. [Five-page DSP note](paper/exact-shift-add-for-dsp.pdf) · [Hardware comparison design](MATCHED_HARDWARE.md) · [Control implementation](src/matched_panel.py) · [Paired records](results/matched-panel/) · [Totals](results/matched-hardware.json)

To check portability to actual Verilog, we froze the two resyn2 area winners per pair, kept all 29 inputs and switched only the logic frontend and output interface. Icarus read the emitted signed RTL, including the real tap signs and shifts; ABC mapped to the same library. Aggregate area changed from 27,801.52 to 26,774.52, 3.694% lower. Seventeen blocks shrank, two tied, and ten grew. Delay here compares those same area-selected graphs, whereas the matched delay figures above select the fastest graph on each side. Each mapped pair of 32-bit signed outputs passed every signed 16-bit input. [Transfer program](src/rtl_transfer.py) · [All transfer outcomes](results/rtl-transfer.json)

Claude wrote a third modular search and reproduced all 25 five-gate exclusions it tested, covering 24 FFT pairs drawn with a fixed seed and one deliberately chosen late-target case. Claude also checked the integer circuits for VINOD03_26B and YEUNG04_40. The third search is outside this repository. The two C++ searches included here cover all 851 six-gate FFT pairs and both FIR lower bounds.

Claude independently rebuilt all 73 DSP proof modules with Lean 4.19.0 and checked the printed axioms. The third search on YEUNG04_40 stopped after about seven hours without a result. The two FIR lower bounds rely on the two completed C++ searches.

The hardware panel contains all 29 six-gate pairs in the 128-point table. Hcub attains six gates on 26 of them. In the matched resyn2 arm the two selected area winners also use six gates on all 26. They total 20,244.20 versus 19,559.50 library units, saving 684.70 of the panel's 995.66 (68.77%). In the standard arm, 24 pairs selected six-gate winners on both sides; they contribute 557.24 of 1,088.70 (51.18%). These observations isolate equal-count pairs. They do not separate the contributions of label choice and wiring. To recompute this split from the paired records, minimize `(area, number of gate rows, graph ID)` within each side of the matched group, then sum the area over records whose two winning costs are six.

## Relation to published results

Dempster and Macleod had already considered optimal two-constant multiplication. Qureshi and Gustafsson had already optimized low-complexity FFT twiddles under accuracy constraints. Fiege, Kumm and Zipf optimize both adder count and bit-level cost with SAT. Our baseline is the released SPIRAL heuristic with 32 seeds. The reported area deltas therefore compare two specified design portfolios. The experiment provides no head-to-head area claim against those bit-level exact optimizers. [MAG2, ISCAS 2005](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCAS.2005.1464962) · [Qureshi and Gustafsson, 2011](https://globals.ieice.org/en_transactions/fundamentals/10.1587/transfun.E94.A.2361/_p) · [Fiege et al., TCAS-I 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSI.2023.3327814) · [Authors' software and research data](https://doi.org/10.48662/daks-21)

Cantaloube et al., section 5, give the earlier 81/83 figure under a one-hour solver limit. Their experiment reports that all solved cases used at most four additional nodes. Our five-extra-node lower bound for VINOD, on the same vectors, puts it outside that set. The paper's aggregated results alone do not identify the other instance. A separate two-second run of their original code solved 73/83. All 73 agree with the unrestricted optimum, and the other ten timed out. These are different experiments. [CP 2026, sections 3 and 5](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CP.2026.11) · [Two-second comparison](results/cp-bounded-comparison.json)

The numerical answers and circuits can be used as regression tests even if a prior result is subsequently found. A benchmark answer with a matching unrestricted lower bound is stronger than an isolated feasible cost. The literature comparison to date does not establish priority for individual historical filter costs.

## Direct comparison with the jMCM deposit

The two pinned upstream CSVs include original signed taps and complete per-method results. We read the taps again and joined by the complete normalized target vector. The 83 CP rows have 81 distinct vectors. Of these, 79 have a full-precision jMCM result, representing 81 named CP rows. DEMPSTER04_E3 and DEMPSTER04_F3 have only truncated rows in that deposit. A different name for the MARTINEZ02_4 vector also appears there. Each vector contributes once. [Original vectors](https://github.com/remi-garcia/jMCM/blob/d4a966a8f3ea4763ae50041ec8e03ea5369aad02/benchmarks/benchmarks.csv) · [Original results](https://github.com/remi-garcia/jMCM/blob/d4a966a8f3ea4763ae50041ec8e03ea5369aad02/benchmarks/results_all.csv)

The comparison uses the fewest adders among all full-precision exact-output MCM and MCM-Bits entries for each vector. The plain adder-count arm gives the same per-vector costs. The deposit totals 956 additions and the unrestricted minima total 899. There are 23 strict improvements and 56 equalities. Every strict improvement corresponds to an asterisk and a time around 1,800 seconds in the plain jMCM arm. VINOD03_26B improves from 20 to 18 and YEUNG04_40 from 28 to 24. These comparisons quantify improvement over feasible solutions recorded in a historical run. [Join and checks](src/compare_jmcm.py) · [Per-vector results](results/jmcm-comparison.json)

The authors set the solver time limit to 1,800 seconds. A single asterisk means the optimization status was not optimal, and a double asterisk marks a discrepancy between the valid graph objective and the solver objective. None of the 23 comparisons overturns a published proof of optimality. ## Eleven filter values already in the SAT benchmark

Fiege, Kumm and Zipf deposited a SAT-MCM benchmark in 2023. Its `benchmark/inputs/mcm/mcm.csv` contains eleven full coefficient vectors and an integer cost field for each. All eleven vectors occur in our CP ledger and all eleven recorded costs equal our unrestricted minima. The values were already public. Neither VINOD03_26B nor YEUNG04_40 is among those eleven vectors. This check does not establish priority for the other filter results or establish which lower bounds the earlier authors proved. To repeat the join, download the paper's original `sat_mcm.tar` and run `python3 src/check_sat_archive.py /path/to/sat_mcm.tar`. The script pins the archive's SHA-256 and never extracts files onto disk.
