# Extending the hardware benchmarks

The [three-page width-bounds note](../width-bounds/paper/ONE_HELPER_NOTE.pdf) left open the exact costs for two CP filter benchmarks and 851 pairs of FFT coefficients. This experiment tests those two filters, VINOD03_26B and YEUNG04_40, and all 851 pairs from Arm’s Q15 tables for FFT lengths 16 through 4096. A pair represents two exact signed-integer coefficient products of one input. Costs count shift/add gates with free signs and exact shifts. Table counts do not measure FFT run time.

1. Compare FIR costs with the previous integer bounds and Hcub seeds 1 through 32. Prove negative results using unrestricted-width modular relaxations, and replay integer witnesses gate by gate.
2. Test the five-gate threshold on every unresolved FFT pair. Use the 983 previously certified five-gate circuits as positive controls. Supply a second implementation or independently checked exclusions and a negative test that deliberately removes a legal operation.
3. Seek six-gate integer circuits for all unresolved pairs. Report the complete denominator, also where the lower and upper bounds differ. Compare with the best of Hcub seeds 1 through 32.
4. For a hardware check, select every previously unresolved distinct pair from the **128-point** Q15 table. Use every member of that panel, including ties. Compare the best standard-cell area, and separately the best delay, among all distinct complete Hcub seed circuits with the circuits obtained here. Use the same 16-bit signed inputs, full exact scalar products, ABC, Nangate45 and two fixed mapping flows for both sides. Include exhaustive arithmetic equivalence of every reported mapped design. Report aggregate area and delay as a panel of separate fixed circuits, not as a complete FFT chip.

The Q15 five-gate classification addresses a different budget from the one-helper bound in the width-bounds note. The practical output is an optimality lookup for fixed-coefficient arithmetic and a correctness corpus for optimizers. No claim of fundamental novelty, measured power, fabricated hardware or general FFT throughput follows from this protocol.
